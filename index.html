The seminar

Understanding a model
from the inside.

A graduate seminar on mechanistic interpretability and its connections to philosophy of mind. We will examine attempts to explain neural networks by identifying their internal representations, circuits, and algorithms.

The recurring question: when does identifying a computational mechanism warrant a psychological interpretation of what a system is doing?

Provisional reading guide

The proposed schedule combines historical and technical foundations with interpretability research and philosophical readings. Weekly selections remain provisional; assessment and meeting details are still to be settled.

Companion course: AI Ethics

01 · Provisional schedule

From foundations to interpretation

A proposed 15-week sequence. The opening week supplies historical background; two weeks develop neural-network fundamentals and backpropagation before we turn to transformers and interpretability.

The mathematical groundwork is introduced through worked examples. Philosophical texts are paired with the investigations they help assess. Selections and optional material indicate the intended reading load; this schedule remains open to revision.

  1. Week 1

    Background and history

    Symbolic AI, connectionism, deep learning, and the shift from evaluating behaviour to investigating internal mechanisms.

    Historical background

    • Goodfellow, Bengio & Courville, Deep Learning (2016), §1.2, “Historical Trends in Deep Learning.”

    Philosophical reading

    • Turing, Computing Machinery and Intelligence (1950), §§1–2 and 6–7.

    Question: What would behavioural success establish about a machine, and what further questions would it leave open?

  2. Week 2

    Technical foundations I: neural networks

    Inputs, weights, biases, activations, hidden layers, nonlinearities, depth, and the forward pass. Introduce the required vector and matrix notation through small examples.

    Technical reading

    • Nielsen, Neural Networks and Deep Learning, chapter 1: “Perceptrons,” “Sigmoid neurons,” and “The architecture of neural networks.”
    • Sanderson, But what is a Neural Network? (3Blue1Brown), as visual support.

    In seminar

    Calculate the activations of a small multilayer network by hand. Distinguish a learned parameter from an activation and compare linear and nonlinear networks.

  3. Week 3

    Technical foundations II: learning and backpropagation

    Targets, loss functions, gradient descent, the chain rule, and backpropagation. Backpropagation computes gradients; the optimisation rule uses them to update parameters.

    Technical reading

    • Nielsen, chapter 1, “Learning with gradient descent,” and chapter 2, through “The backpropagation algorithm.” Proofs and code are optional.
    • Optional historical reading: Rumelhart, Hinton & Williams, Learning representations by back-propagating errors (1986).
    • Visual support: 3Blue1Brown, What is backpropagation really doing?.

    In seminar

    Work through one forward pass, backward pass, and parameter update in a tiny network. Distinguish explaining how a model was trained from explaining a particular computation it performs.

  4. Week 4

    Transformers and levels of explanation

    Tokens, embeddings, attention, multilayer perceptrons, residual streams, and next-token prediction.

    Technical reading

    • 3Blue1Brown, Transformers, the tech behind LLMs and Attention in transformers, step-by-step.
    • Reference: Elhage et al., A Mathematical Framework for Transformer Circuits, the architecture and residual-stream discussion.

    Philosophical reading

    • Marr, Vision (1982), chapter 1, §1.2, “Understanding Complex Information-Processing Systems.”

    Question: How do a model’s task, algorithm, and implementation relate? Where does mechanistic interpretability fit within Marr’s framework?

  5. Week 5

    Features, circuits, and mechanisms

    Feature visualisation, connected components, and the ambition to explain a network by explaining its parts.

    Technical reading

    • Olah et al., Zoom In: An Introduction to Circuits (2020).

    Philosophical reading

    • Machamer, Darden & Craver, Thinking about Mechanisms (2000).

    Question: What would make a neural-network circuit a mechanism in the philosophical sense? Which parts of the analogy with biological mechanisms hold?

  6. Week 6

    Transformer circuits and induction heads

    Attention-head composition and a concrete candidate mechanism for learning from context.

    Technical reading

    • Elhage et al., A Mathematical Framework for Transformer Circuits (2021), selections on attention-head composition and induction heads.
    • Olsson et al., In-context Learning and Induction Heads (2022), overview and principal lines of evidence; detailed derivations optional.

    Philosophical reading

    • Revisit Marr’s three levels and Machamer, Darden & Craver’s account of mechanisms.

    Question: What distinguishes identifying a copying algorithm from explaining the broader capacity to learn from context?

  7. Week 7

    Causal interventions and the IOI circuit

    Ablation, activation patching, and the faithfulness, completeness, and minimality of an explanation.

    Technical reading

    • Wang et al., Interpretability in the Wild: a Circuit for Indirect Object Identification in GPT-2 small (2022/2023), main paper; appendices optional.

    Philosophical reading

    • Woodward, Causation and Manipulability, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, §§4–5 and 9 on interventions, causal models, and counterfactuals.
    • Optional: Woodward, Making Things Happen (2003), for the fuller account; library access may be required.

    Question: Which interventions support a causal explanation, and when might an intervention change the system in a way that makes the result misleading?

  8. Week 8

    Grokking and computational implementation

    Reverse-engineering an algorithm for modular arithmetic. Introduce the Fourier ideas needed to follow the case study.

    Technical reading

    • Nanda et al., Progress measures for grokking via mechanistic interpretability (2023), main argument and recovered algorithm; technical appendices optional.

    Philosophical reading

    • Chalmers, Does a Rock Implement Every Finite-State Automaton? (1996), focusing on the implementation problem, counterfactual constraints, and causal organisation.

    Question: What makes it true that a network implements the recovered algorithm? Why is fitting a description to its observed outputs insufficient?

  9. Week 9

    Superposition and the structure of thought

    Distributed representations, polysemantic neurons, and the distinction between features and dimensions.

    Technical reading

    • Elhage et al., Toy Models of Superposition (2022), basic model and superposition results; advanced geometry optional.

    Philosophical reading

    • Fodor & Pylyshyn, Connectionism and Cognitive Architecture: A Critical Analysis (1988), §§3.1–3.3 on productivity, systematicity, and compositionality.

    Question: What demands does systematic thought place on representations? How much does evidence about superposition tell us about those demands?

  10. Week 10

    Sparse autoencoders and levels of representation

    Dictionary learning, interpretable features, and feature splitting. Examine what the choice of decomposition contributes to an interpretation.

    Technical reading

    • Bricken et al., Towards Monosemanticity (2023), problem setup, selected feature investigations, and feature splitting.

    Philosophical reading

    • Smolensky, On the proper treatment of connectionism (1988), target article, pp. 1–23, focusing on conceptual and subconceptual levels; commentaries optional. Reading copy.

    Question: Should extracted features be identified with concepts, subconceptual representations, or something else? How does this bear on the previous week’s challenge?

  11. Week 11

    Othello and the case for representation

    Decoding board states, intervening on internal activity, and distinguishing a model’s representations from a researcher’s descriptions.

    Technical reading

    • Li et al., Emergent World Representations: Exploring a Sequence Model Trained on a Synthetic Task (2022/2023), main paper.

    Philosophical reading

    • Ramsey, Representation Reconsidered (2007), chapter 1, §1.2, “The job description challenge.” Book chapter; use library access.

    Question: What explanatory work must a state perform to count as a representation? Does the Othello evidence meet that requirement?

  12. Week 12

    Causal abstraction and functionalism

    Relating a higher-level model to a neural network through interventions, with attention to what a faithful abstraction preserves.

    Technical reading

    • Geiger et al., Causal Abstraction: A Theoretical Foundation for Mechanistic Interpretability (2023; revised 2025), introduction and worked examples; formal generalisations optional.

    Philosophical reading

    • Putnam, The Nature of Mental States (originally published as “Psychological Predicates,” 1967).

    Question: How does establishing a higher-level causal organisation bear on functionalist claims? What further argument would be needed to identify that organisation with a mental state?

  13. Week 13

    Scaling interpretability and real patterns

    Feature extraction in a production model, granularity, and what steering experiments establish.

    Technical reading

    • Templeton et al., Scaling Monosemanticity (2024), selected feature and steering case studies, plus limitations.

    Philosophical reading

    • Dennett, Real Patterns (1991).

    Question: When is an interpretation tracking an objective pattern? How should predictive usefulness, compression, and causal evidence contribute to that judgement?

  14. Week 14

    Circuit tracing and psychological interpretation

    Attribution graphs, approximate replacement models, and claims about planning or reasoning.

    Technical reading

    • Ameisen et al., Circuit Tracing (2025), method overview and validation.
    • Lindsey et al., On the Biology of a Large Language Model (2025), one planning or multistep-reasoning case study.

    Philosophical reading

    • Revisit Dennett, Real Patterns, and Chalmers on implementation.

    Question: What does a graph of computation establish about reasoning or planning, and what depends on the validity of the replacement model and our psychological vocabulary?

  15. Week 15

    Limits, criticism, and synthesis

    Robustness, alternative interpretations, and standards of evidence for mechanistic and psychological claims.

    Technical reading

    • Li et al., Interpretability Illusions with Sparse Autoencoders (2025), main findings.
    • Optional overview: Sharkey et al., Open Problems in Mechanistic Interpretability (2025), selections on evaluation and applications.

    Philosophical reading

    • Williams et al., Mechanistic Interpretability Needs Philosophy (2025; revised 2026).

    Question: What would a successful science of mechanistic interpretability explain? Which philosophical questions can its methods help settle, and which require additional arguments?

02 · Foundations and case studies

An emerging canon

Mechanistic interpretability has a recognizable core, especially within the circuits tradition. This is a proposed selection, with an emphasis on transformers; the field’s methods and standards of evidence are still developing. Some foundational contributions appeared as substantial web research articles.

A reference guide to the core papers in the schedule above. Each paper is paired with a question for philosophical discussion.

  1. 01

    Olah et al. · 2020 · Distill

    Zoom In: An Introduction to Circuits

    The circuits research programme, introduced through vision networks: explain behaviour by studying features and the connections between them.

    Discuss What makes a collection of components an explanatory mechanism?

  2. 02

    Elhage et al. · 2021 · Transformer Circuits

    A Mathematical Framework for Transformer Circuits

    Introduces a framework for analysing information flow and attention-head composition, using small attention-only transformers.

    Discuss At what level should we describe the computation a network performs?

  3. 03

    Olsson et al. · 2022 · Transformer Circuits

    In-context Learning and Induction Heads

    Investigates a sequence-copying mechanism and its relationship to learning from context. The evidence is causal in small attention-only models and correlational in larger models.

    Discuss What licenses generalising a mechanistic explanation across models and tasks?

  4. 04

    Wang et al. · 2022 preprint · ICLR 2023

    Interpretability in the Wild: a Circuit for Indirect Object Identification in GPT-2 small

    A detailed investigation of a language task using causal interventions, evaluated for faithfulness, completeness, and minimality.

    Discuss When does a successful intervention establish that an explanation is correct?

  5. 05

    Elhage et al. · 2022 · Transformer Circuits

    Toy Models of Superposition

    Shows how toy networks can represent more features than they have dimensions, helping explain why individual neurons can respond to multiple features.

    Discuss What are the units of representation when features do not line up with neurons?

  6. 06

    Bricken et al. · 2023 · Transformer Circuits

    Towards Monosemanticity: Decomposing Language Models With Dictionary Learning

    Uses sparse autoencoders to extract relatively interpretable features from a small transformer, offering a different unit of analysis from individual neurons.

    Discuss Are we discovering the model’s concepts, or constructing a useful description of its activity?

  7. 07

    Nanda et al. · 2023 · ICLR

    Progress measures for grokking via mechanistic interpretability

    Reverse-engineers a Fourier-based algorithm for modular addition and uses it to track changes during training.

    Discuss What counts as evidence that a neural network implements a particular algorithm?

  8. 08

    Li et al. · 2022 preprint · ICLR 2023

    Emergent World Representations: Exploring a Sequence Model Trained on a Synthetic Task

    The Othello study: evidence for internal board-state representations in a model trained on move sequences, including interventions on those representations.

    Discuss What distinguishes representing a world from tracking patterns in a sequence?

For technical background and optional practical work, ARENA’s Transformer Interpretability curriculum includes exercises on induction heads, indirect object identification, superposition, grokking, and OthelloGPT.

03 · Further reading

Extensions and challenges

Readings that extend the foundational cases and sharpen questions about what the methods establish.

Geiger et al. · 2023; revised 2025

Causal Abstraction: A Theoretical Foundation for Mechanistic Interpretability

A framework for testing whether a higher-level explanation faithfully captures a model’s causal structure. A bridge to functionalism and levels of explanation.

Templeton et al. · 2024

Scaling Monosemanticity: Extracting Interpretable Features from Claude 3 Sonnet

Extends sparse feature extraction to a production language model. Raises questions about feature labels, granularity, and the reach of steering experiments.

Ameisen et al.; Lindsey et al. · 2025

Circuit Tracing and On the Biology of a Large Language Model

A method and companion case studies tracing computation through an approximate replacement model. How faithfully do those graphs explain the original system?

Sharkey et al. · 2025

Open Problems in Mechanistic Interpretability

A field-wide review of conceptual, practical, and social challenges. Useful for assessing the distance between successful case studies and broader scientific ambitions.

Li et al. · 2025

Interpretability Illusions with Sparse Autoencoders: Evaluating Robustness of Concept Representations

Tests the robustness of SAE interpretations under input perturbations. A challenge to treating an intuitive concept label as sufficient evidence of a faithful interpretation.

Williams et al. · 2025; revised 2026

Mechanistic Interpretability Needs Philosophy

Argues for philosophical work on the concepts, assumptions, and explanatory strategies of mechanistic interpretability.

04 · Philosophy of mind

Questions to carry
through the readings

Representation and content
What makes an internal state a representation of something? How do we distinguish information that can be decoded from information the model uses?
Functionalism and realisation
When does a network implement a higher-level computation? Could different internal organisations realise the same cognitive capacity?
Connectionism and compositionality
How are concepts combined in distributed representations? Do discovered features support familiar accounts of the structure of thought?
Psychological attribution
What additional evidence would justify interpreting a mechanism in terms of belief, understanding, or reasoning?
