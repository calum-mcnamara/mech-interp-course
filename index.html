The seminar

Understanding a model
from the inside.

A graduate seminar on mechanistic interpretability and its connections to philosophy of mind. We will examine attempts to explain neural networks by identifying their internal representations, circuits, and algorithms.

The recurring question: when does identifying a computational mechanism warrant a psychological interpretation of what a system is doing?

Provisional reading guide

The selections below are a starting point for developing the seminar. Weekly readings, assessment, and meeting details remain to be settled.

Companion course: AI Ethics

01 · Foundations and case studies

An emerging canon

Mechanistic interpretability has a recognizable core, especially within the circuits tradition. This is a proposed selection, with an emphasis on transformers; the field’s methods and standards of evidence are still developing. Some foundational contributions appeared as substantial web research articles.

A suggested reading order. Each paper is paired with a question for philosophical discussion.

  1. 01

    Olah et al. · 2020 · Distill

    Zoom In: An Introduction to Circuits

    The circuits research programme, introduced through vision networks: explain behaviour by studying features and the connections between them.

    Discuss What makes a collection of components an explanatory mechanism?

  2. 02

    Elhage et al. · 2021 · Transformer Circuits

    A Mathematical Framework for Transformer Circuits

    Introduces a framework for analysing information flow and attention-head composition, using small attention-only transformers.

    Discuss At what level should we describe the computation a network performs?

  3. 03

    Olsson et al. · 2022 · Transformer Circuits

    In-context Learning and Induction Heads

    Investigates a sequence-copying mechanism and its relationship to learning from context. The evidence is causal in small attention-only models and correlational in larger models.

    Discuss What licenses generalising a mechanistic explanation across models and tasks?

  4. 04

    Wang et al. · 2022 preprint · ICLR 2023

    Interpretability in the Wild: a Circuit for Indirect Object Identification in GPT-2 small

    A detailed investigation of a language task using causal interventions, evaluated for faithfulness, completeness, and minimality.

    Discuss When does a successful intervention establish that an explanation is correct?

  5. 05

    Elhage et al. · 2022 · Transformer Circuits

    Toy Models of Superposition

    Shows how toy networks can represent more features than they have dimensions, helping explain why individual neurons can respond to multiple features.

    Discuss What are the units of representation when features do not line up with neurons?

  6. 06

    Bricken et al. · 2023 · Transformer Circuits

    Towards Monosemanticity: Decomposing Language Models With Dictionary Learning

    Uses sparse autoencoders to extract relatively interpretable features from a small transformer, offering a different unit of analysis from individual neurons.

    Discuss Are we discovering the model’s concepts, or constructing a useful description of its activity?

  7. 07

    Nanda et al. · 2023 · ICLR

    Progress measures for grokking via mechanistic interpretability

    Reverse-engineers a Fourier-based algorithm for modular addition and uses it to track changes during training.

    Discuss What counts as evidence that a neural network implements a particular algorithm?

  8. 08

    Li et al. · 2022 preprint · ICLR 2023

    Emergent World Representations: Exploring a Sequence Model Trained on a Synthetic Task

    The Othello study: evidence for internal board-state representations in a model trained on move sequences, including interventions on those representations.

    Discuss What distinguishes representing a world from tracking patterns in a sequence?

For technical background and optional practical work, ARENA’s Transformer Interpretability curriculum includes exercises on induction heads, indirect object identification, superposition, grokking, and OthelloGPT.

02 · Further reading

Extensions and challenges

Readings that extend the foundational cases and sharpen questions about what the methods establish.

Geiger et al. · 2023; revised 2025

Causal Abstraction: A Theoretical Foundation for Mechanistic Interpretability

A framework for testing whether a higher-level explanation faithfully captures a model’s causal structure. A bridge to functionalism and levels of explanation.

Templeton et al. · 2024

Scaling Monosemanticity: Extracting Interpretable Features from Claude 3 Sonnet

Extends sparse feature extraction to a production language model. Raises questions about feature labels, granularity, and the reach of steering experiments.

Ameisen et al.; Lindsey et al. · 2025

Circuit Tracing and On the Biology of a Large Language Model

A method and companion case studies tracing computation through an approximate replacement model. How faithfully do those graphs explain the original system?

Sharkey et al. · 2025

Open Problems in Mechanistic Interpretability

A field-wide review of conceptual, practical, and social challenges. Useful for assessing the distance between successful case studies and broader scientific ambitions.

Li et al. · 2025

Interpretability Illusions with Sparse Autoencoders: Evaluating Robustness of Concept Representations

Tests the robustness of SAE interpretations under input perturbations. A challenge to treating an intuitive concept label as sufficient evidence of a faithful interpretation.

Williams et al. · 2025; revised 2026

Mechanistic Interpretability Needs Philosophy

Argues for philosophical work on the concepts, assumptions, and explanatory strategies of mechanistic interpretability.

03 · Philosophy of mind

Questions to carry
through the readings

Representation and content
What makes an internal state a representation of something? How do we distinguish information that can be decoded from information the model uses?
Functionalism and realisation
When does a network implement a higher-level computation? Could different internal organisations realise the same cognitive capacity?
Connectionism and compositionality
How are concepts combined in distributed representations? Do discovered features support familiar accounts of the structure of thought?
Psychological attribution
What additional evidence would justify interpreting a mechanism in terms of belief, understanding, or reasoning?
